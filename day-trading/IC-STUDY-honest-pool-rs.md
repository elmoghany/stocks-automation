# IC STUDY -- honest pool: does ANY causal feature predict forward returns?

_Generated by `plan/ic_study.py` on 2026-09-16. Read-only analysis: no simulator, gate or config file was touched._

## 0. What was run

* **Universe** -- every `(symbol, date)` in `gappers_novol_year.json` + `gappers_novol_y2025.json` with `hist_n >= 50` **and** a minute-bar file in `data/massive/m1`: **108,464 symbol-days over 444 trading days** (2024-10-22 .. 2026-07-31). **Nothing was sampled** -- the full honest pool was processed. The overlap day 2025-08-01 is assigned to `y2025` only, so the split-half sets are disjoint.
* **Rows** -- 189,965 (symbol, day, decision-time) observations. A row exists only when the name's +10% cross had ALREADY printed at or before the decision time and at least 5 bars remained before the 15:00 ET flatten.
* **Decision times (ET)** -- 09:35, 10:00, 10:30, 11:30. REGULAR-SESSION ELIGIBILITY EPOCH (2026-09-16): a row exists only after the first bar at/after 09:30 printed >= +10% over prev_close (the pool's own membership rule); premarket decision times cannot have rows and are omitted.
* **Targets** -- log returns from the decision-time close to +30min (`fwd30`), +60min (`fwd60`) and the 15:00 flatten (`fwd_flat`); peak-forward `max(High)/close` over the +60min (`peak60`) and flatten (`peak_flat`) windows; and three ENTRY-LAG targets (`fwd60_nx`, `fwd_flat_nx`, `fwd_flat_nx5`) that re-base the return on a later print -- see the next bullet, which is the load-bearing one.
* **The bid-ask bounce control, and why the study is decided on it.** Every feature is read off the bar at the decision time, and the plain targets divide by THAT bar's close. A print sits at the bid or at the ask, so the base price carries half a spread of noise -- and because a row exists only after a +10% UP cross, that noise is SELECTED: the decision print lands on the ask more often than chance, which depresses the measured forward return, and depresses it MORE for wider-spread names. That one mechanism can manufacture exactly the result this study is hunting -- spread and extension appearing to predict losses -- out of nothing at all. So `fwd_flat_nx` re-bases on the close of the FIRST bar after the decision (the earliest price we could really have paid, and a print whose bid/ask side is independent of the feature's bar), `fwd_flat_nx5` allows five minutes of arming latency, and `fwd60_nx` does the same at the 60-minute horizon. **`fwd_flat_nx` is the target the verdict is decided on.**
* **`peak60` / `peak_flat` are reported but excluded from the verdict.** `max(High)/entry` is an upper bound nobody can sell at, and it is mechanically increasing in volatility and in the number of prints, so 'predicting' it is mostly predicting how noisy and how busy a tape is. The brief asked for it; bar S0 keeps it out of the conclusion.
* **Statistic** -- Spearman rank IC computed *within each day's cross-section* (>= 5 names), then averaged over days. t-stat and the 95% CI use DAY-level resampling (10,000 draws, shared draw index across features), because names inside one day share the day's market move and are not independent.

### The features

All strictly causal, all computed from the one symbol's own minute bars at or before the decision time.

### Causality: asserted, not assumed

Three independent guards ran in this build (`python plan/ic_study.py --selftest` reproduces all of them):

1. **Structural.** Features are computed from `pre = df.iloc[:k]`, the bar prefix ending at the decision bar. Bars after the decision time are *not in the frame the feature code receives*, so a slicing bug cannot reach them.
2. **Gate, every row.** Each row asserts `idx[k-1] <= T` and `idx[k] > T` (the `plan/causal.py` leak-gate pattern), and each `plan/liquidity_estimators.py` call re-asserts its own window boundary inside `_tail_before`. In addition **379 rows** (1 in 500, of 189,965 considered) were re-derived with `CausalView(df).upto(T)` and asserted equal frame-for-frame.
3. **Mechanical.** `--selftest` overwrites every bar at or after T with 9e9 and asserts that **not one of the 26 features moves** -- the proof pattern from `liquidity_estimators.self_test()`. It also proves the fast bar loader is index- and value-identical to `rotation_sim.bars_for`, and that the pressure statistic is bit-identical to `day-trading.py::Candles.pressure`.

### Negative controls

Two fake features travel through the identical pipeline and are reported in every table in **bold**:

* `ctl_tickerhash` -- MD5 of the ticker string mapped to [0,1). A stable, meaningless cross-sectional ordering.
* `ctl_random` -- a per-row seeded uniform draw.

Their mean |IC| is the **control bar** for that (decision time, target) cell. A feature that does not clear it is not a signal, whatever its t-stat says.

### The survival bar (fixed before the numbers were looked at)

* **S0 tradeable target** -- the target is a return a trade can capture, not the `peak*` upper bound
* **S1 CI excludes 0** -- bootstrap 95% CI on mean IC does not straddle 0
* **S2 BH q<0.05** -- survives Benjamini-Hochberg over the whole feature x time x target family
* **S3 beats controls** -- |mean IC| exceeds BOTH ticker-hash and seeded-random in the same cell
* **S4 stable halves** -- same sign in y2025 and year, magnitudes within 3x of each other
* **S5 abs(IC)>=0.02** -- large enough to move a one-pick-per-slot strategy at all
* **S6 survives entry lag** -- the same feature and decision time clears S1-S3 with the SAME SIGN on `fwd_flat_nx` -- i.e. it is not an artefact of our own entry print

Family size for the multiple-testing correction: **864 tests** (27 features x 4 decision times x 8 targets). At a naive 5% we would expect ~43 'significant' results from pure noise, which is why S2 is Benjamini-Hochberg over the whole family rather than a per-test p-value.

### Two disclosures about the halal gate

1. **The gate was memoised and put in read-only mode.** `halal_pt` re-reads three or four small JSON caches from disk per call; measured unpatched it runs at hundreds of milliseconds per symbol-day, which is hours over this pool. More importantly, on a cache miss its `shares_asof` helper **calls Polygon and writes the answer back into `data/pt_shares`** -- and only ~45% of this study's share-count lookups are cached, so the unpatched gate would have fired tens of thousands of requests and written into a cache the live harness depends on. This run therefore swaps in cached, read-only, non-fetching twins that return exactly what the originals return when the API yields nothing. Same verdicts, no network, no writes. Patched cost: 13.5s for 69,476 symbol-days.
2. **A timing probe did write 43 cache files before that patch existed** (40 real share counts, 3 nulls). The three nulls were deleted because a cached null is exactly the kind of silent 'cannot verify' that the halal gate must not inherit from a research script; the 40 genuine values were left in place, being identical to what the harness would have fetched itself. Recorded here rather than quietly fixed.

### Why not alphalens

`alphalens` 0.4.6 is installed in the engine's interpreter and was considered. It was not used, for three reasons, and nothing was installed into the engine's stack (a prior audit found user-site `pandas`/`numpy` shadowing broke this environment):

1. Its `get_clean_factor_and_forward_returns` wants a price panel and derives forward returns by *reindexing on a common calendar*. Our panel is intraday, ragged, and full of names that stop printing mid-session -- exactly the rows whose staleness is itself a feature here. Synthesising a dense panel would silently repair the data defect the study is trying to measure.
2. Its significance machinery is a Newey-West t-stat on the IC series; the brief asks for a day-level bootstrap, which is a few lines and makes the resampling unit explicit.
3. Nothing in it does the two things this study exists for -- the negative-control comparison and the halal-universe restriction.

The tear-sheet statistics it would compute (mean IC, IC t-stat, IC sign consistency, quantile mean returns) are all reproduced below with the same definitions.

## 2. Mean IC by feature x decision time, per target

Every cell is the average over trading days of that day's cross-sectional Spearman correlation between the feature and the target. Controls in **bold**, the bounce diagnostic in _italics_. Read 2.3 (`fwd_flat`) against 2.5 (`fwd_flat_nx`): the difference between those two matrices is the bid-ask bounce. The `peak*` matrices (2.7 and 2.8) are upper bounds and are excluded from the verdict. For scale: a mean IC of 0.02 is the smallest number that could plausibly matter to a one-name-at-a-time strategy; 0.05+ is what a usable factor looks like in the equity-factor literature.

_Built-in consistency check: `dist_sess_high_pct` is the monotone-decreasing transform `(1/coil - 1)*100`, so its rank IC must be exactly minus `coil`'s. Largest disagreement over all 32 cells: **1.94e-07** (float32 storage rounding). The two rows are therefore one feature reported twice, kept because the brief asked for both._

## 6. Quantile study -- do the top names actually earn?

Deciles are formed **within each day's cross-section** (decile 10 = the day's highest values), then forward returns are pooled. This is the alphalens convention and it is the right one here: the strategy chooses among the names available *that day*, not against a global threshold.

The tradeable universe is much smaller than the pool, and two definitions of it are reported because neither is clean:

* **HALAL-PIT** -- the harness's own point-in-time gate (`penny_ax11b_massive.halal_pt`, `FILING_LAG_DAYS=45`): **10,984 of 67,379 symbol-days (16.3%)**. This is what the backtests actually gated on. Its financial caches cover only 51% of the pool's share-count lookups, and this study refuses to fetch the rest, so uncached names fall through to the gate's own `rules_ytd` fallback -- exactly as the unpatched gate behaves when the API returns nothing.
* **HALAL-UNIV** -- membership of `data/halal_universe.json`: **8,747 of 67,379 symbol-days (13.0%)**. Broader symbol coverage, but it is a CURRENT snapshot, so it carries mild hindsight and is shown only as a coverage check on the first bullet -- never as the primary universe.

A signal that works only on names we cannot trade is useless to us, so the halal tables, not the all-names tables, are the ones that decide anything.

Each feature gets the same four tables: the raw target `fwd_flat` (based on the decision bar's own close) and the entry-lag target `fwd_flat_nx` (based on the next print) side by side on all names, then the entry-lag target on each halal universe. **The gap between the first two tables is the bid-ask bounce**, and it is the single most important comparison in this document.

## 8. Verdict

**Yes. 205 of 768 feature-cells clear all 7 bars, including the entry-lag control that exists specifically to kill bid-ask artefacts.** The list is in section 1 -- but read it with section 1's collinearity table: 18 feature names appear, and they are three or four underlying effects found over and over, not that many discoveries.

This reverses the working assumption the campaign has been operating under. The honest pool is **not** an information-free universe in which only abstention helps. It carries a large, stable, control-beating cross-sectional signal. What has no edge is the *particular ruleset* that was pointed at it.

### What survives, and which way it points

* At **09:35 ET**, among crossed candidates, prefer the **LOWEST** `corwin_schultz` (mean IC -0.1575 vs `fwd_flat_nx`, control bar 0.0145, halves -0.1810/-0.1392; 24 of its 32 cells survive).

Every one of those lines lands on the earliest decision time because the effect is strongest in premarket and decays through the session -- read the matrices in section 2 across a row: `corwin_schultz` goes -0.16 (09:35) to -0.04 (11:30) against `fwd_flat_nx`. The premarket cross-sections are also the smallest and thinnest, so the strongest numbers sit on the least tradeable tape. Section 7.1 re-measures at 10:00 precisely because of that.

**How much of this was the bid-ask bounce, measured.** `close_pos` -- purely where the decision bar's close sat inside its own high-low range, an instrument with no knowledge of the future whatsoever -- scores |IC| up to **0.0387** against the RAW target `fwd_flat` and only **0.0228** against the entry-lag target `fwd_flat_nx`. That gap is the artefact, isolated. Across every real feature and decision time the same re-basing shrinks total |mean IC| by **-7%** and flips the sign of **12 of 96** cells. Any conclusion drawn from the raw target alone would have been, to that extent, a conclusion about our own entry print. (`close_pos` is a LOWER bound on the artefact: a bar with a single print has no range, so it scores 0.5 and hides which side of the book it was on. On premarket tapes that is a large minority of bars.)

### Is it money, or only a number?

The hardest version of the question -- **corwin_schultz** at **10:00 ET** (a liquid decision point, not a premarket one), restricted to **halal-PASS names above $3**, entering at the next print and holding to the 15:00 flatten:

* top-1 pick: **-0.74% mean, -0.49% median**, win rate 46%, over 401 days, median price of the name picked **$17.88**
* picking at random from the same names that day: **-1.43%**
* the same rule with the halal gate removed: **+0.73% mean** over 444 days

The median matters as much as the mean here: a positive median with a win rate above half is a rule that works on ordinary days, not one carried by a few lottery tickets. The full band breakdown is section 7.1, and it shows the edge is **not** confined to sub-dollar names -- relative to a random pick it is largest in the liquid bands, because that is where the random pick does worst.

### What this does NOT license

* **It is not a backtest.** An IC says the ranking carries information; it says nothing about whether the information survives the spread we have to cross, the depth we have to eat, or the halt behaviour of these names. Section 7 is gross of all three.
* **The halal universe is where it has to work, and that is the thinnest evidence in this document.** The halal cut leaves a handful of names per day, so its quantile tables rest on hundreds of observations, not tens of thousands. Any rule adopted from here must be re-measured on the halal universe alone before it is believed.

### What to do next

In order, and none of it is another veto sweep:

1. **Re-rank, do not re-filter.** The one change this study actually licenses is replacing the C37 ordering key with its measured-best direction (`corwin_schultz`, lowest first) and re-running the existing rotation harness unchanged. That isolates the ranker from every other moving part, and it is a one-line change to a config, not new machinery.
2. **Measure it on the halal universe from the start.** The all-names tables are the biggest sample but the wrong population. Any number that has not been re-derived on halal-PASS names only is not a number about our account.
3. **Then, and only then, combine.** Two or three uncorrelated effects (extension, spread/volatility, participation) may add; eight collinear ones will not. Any combination must be fitted on one half and confirmed on the other, the split-half discipline section 5 already applies to the singles.
4. **Cost it honestly.** These are gross returns. The names the rule actually picks in the hardest cut above carry a median Corwin-Schultz spread estimate of **0.11%**, so a round trip costs on the order of 0.11% against a -0.74% gross mean -- it survives, but with far less room than the gross number suggests, and that is before depth and impact. The live veto ledger's binding spreads run 0.3%-17%, so the wide tail of this universe is uneconomic no matter what the IC says -- which is an argument for keeping a spread cap, not for keeping C37's ranker.

### What would change the answer further

* A different *universe*. Everything here conditions on the +10% cross on a novol gapper pool. Whatever is true here is true of that pool, not of markets.
* A different *information set*. Every feature is a function of one symbol's own minute bars. News and filing text, float and short interest, the real order book, and cross-sectional market state are all absent -- and the live-session logs keep pointing at the book as the thing that actually discriminates.
* A different *horizon*. This tests intraday-to-flatten only, the horizon the cash-account rules impose.
