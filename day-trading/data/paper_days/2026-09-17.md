### The premarket entry that was refused -- and the rule ambiguity behind it

At 08:25 VICR cleared, for the first time, every gate that had been blocking it: spread 0.485%
(bid 205.50 / ask 206.50, inside the 0.5% cap), depth 134 sh within trigger+0.5% against 72
needed, and a premarket high of 206.9108 sitting *above* the 206.50 ask -- so a stop-buy was a
real stop, not a disguised market order. Only the size rule was left, and it did not give one
answer:

| reading of "20% of the trailing 10 completed minutes' volume" | value | verdict on a 72-sh ticket |
|---|---|---|
| `bars_paste.py` trail10vol = last 10 **stored** bars (which span 04:01-07:58, four hours) | 1,307 sh -> cap **261** | would ARM |
| wall clock, 08:15-08:25 ET: VICR printed **zero** shares | 0 sh -> cap **0** | REFUSES |

A 25x disagreement, straddling the ticket size. Logged loudly per the no-silent-fallbacks rule
and the conservative branch taken: a name that has not printed a share in 27 minutes is not one
where 72 shares is 20% of anything, the skill's own asymmetry says an unfilled entry is merely a
missed opportunity, and premarket legs are the documented loss centre. **This needs a user
ruling** -- it is a live-vs-backtest parity question, not a style preference, and the current
code is the permissive side.

Honest postscript: VICR traded 206.93 at 08:50, so that stop would have triggered. The
regular-session ORB entry that replaced it did better than the refused premarket one would have.

## Process notes

* **`run_scan` began spilling to a file** once the regular session widened the result set past
  the inline limit. A spilled dump feeds `scan_sweep.py` directly, which removes the
  hand-transcription step entirely -- the exact step the sweep tooling exists to protect. The
  smaller premarket scans still arrived inline and used the `scan_delta.py` incremental path.
* **Clock discipline.** Every `rank` and `trigger` call omitted `--as-of` so the command reads
  its own zoneinfo clock. That structurally removes the hand-typed-clock error class rather
  than relying on care. One cosmetic lapse is logged: the premarket artefact *filenames* were
  typed from guessed clock times and are 3-16 minutes ahead of the truth (`ops_clock_correction`);
  no causal cutoff used them.
* **Heartbeat during long waits.** The standing `wait_until.py` is the clock authority but blocks
  silently for up to 8 minutes, which would breach the 5-minute heartbeat rule. `_d24_wait.py`
  wraps the same zoneinfo clock and the same 480 s cap but beats the flag every 30 s.
