## Lessons
1. **The premarket spread cap is the dominant filter of this strategy as currently run, and it is not
   free.** Across Days 6 and 7 it has now blocked the eventual best-performing halal name twice (KOPN
   +29% on Day 6, SMWB +25% today). It is doing exactly what it was designed to do — refuse entries whose
   execution cannot be bounded — but two sessions of evidence say a 0.5% cap applied to *premarket* books
   behaves very differently from the same cap after 09:30. Worth a backtest: relax the cap pre-09:30 to
   something depth-aware, or defer wide-book names to the open rather than rejecting them outright.
2. **Coil and book quality are anti-correlated in the premarket.** All morning, whichever name had the
   coil and the trusted pressure had the broken book, and whichever had the tight book had lost its coil.
   That is probably structural — a name being actively accumulated premarket has a wide book precisely
   because it is being accumulated — and it means the ranking and the execution filter routinely disagree.
3. **A single non-resolving position is the worst case for C37's economics.** Not a loss, a lockout.
   Seven tickets exist to be recycled; one trade that neither stops nor trails consumes all seven slots'
   worth of session time for one ticket's worth of exposure. The edge lives in the rotation.
4. **The fill-arming rule now has four data points behind it and they are unambiguous.** Both negative
   fill-realism observations came from arming at an already-printed level; both non-negatives came from
   clean forward triggers. Today's 3-cent self-correction is the cheapest possible demonstration.
5. **Delegated sub-agents must be given hard exclusion lists.** A delegated scan re-surfaced two names as
   eligible that had been live-screened and FAILED earlier the same session, because the universe file
   carries no verdict for them. Caught both times, but it is a live risk in the delegation pattern; the
   fix (explicit HARD EXCLUSIONS in the prompt) is now in place.
