# C37 backtest replayed over the four live paper days (2026-08-10 → 08-13)

**Question.** For the four live paper-trading days, what would the adopted C37
config have made on those exact days, versus what live paper trading actually
made? **Hypothesis under test: the backtest would have made materially more —
live execution and gating are leaving money on the table.**

**Verdict: REFUTED, and decisively.** On the three evaluable days the backtest
LOSES more than live did — between **$1,195 and $4,470 worse**, depending on
which halal gate you hold it to. Live's −$227.74 is the *best* of the four
measurements, not the worst. There is no money being left on the table by
execution or gating on these days; if anything the live vetoes and the
one-position rule saved money.

## 2. Two head-to-head cases where live beat the sim on the same tape

**BE, 2026-08-12 — same name, live executed better.** In run C the sim reached
BE and took it at **09:45 @ $243.61**, flattening at 14:59 @ $237.82 for
**−$353.49**. Live armed the premarket-high stop-buy and filled at **09:26 @
$235.37**, exiting 14:57 @ $237.03 for **+$104.58**. Live was 19 minutes earlier
and $8.24 cheaper on entry, on the same day, in the same name. The sim's 5-minute
re-rank cadence and its wait for a rank-1 slot cost it the good entry.

**SMWB, 2026-08-12 — the veto live "paid for" was free.** The Day 7 ledger
records SMWB as the *largest veto cost of the day*: rank 1 for ~15 premarket
cycles, vetoed every cycle on an 8.4-12.4% book, and it then ran +25%. Run C
lets the sim buy it (no book model): entry 07:05 @ $8.38, stopped out 09:43 @
$8.17 for **−$368.03**. C37's exit machinery did not capture the +25% run. The
spread veto did not cost money that day; it avoided a loss.

## 4. Known asymmetries — this is NOT fully apples-to-apples

Stated plainly, including the ones that cut against my own conclusion.

**Favouring the backtest (it should have scored HIGHER than it did):**

1. **No book-depth or spread veto in the sim.** It pays a flat 50bps premarket
   spread haircut and takes every trade. Live refuses any entry with an inside
   book wider than 0.5% and reduces size on thin L2 depth. Live vetoed 3 of 6
   arming decisions on Day 8 alone (50%). The sim faces no such filter — and it
   still lost more. (SMWB above is the concrete instance.)
2. **The sim fills AT the trigger.** Live's Day 5 LFST fill was 1.6% worse than
   the +60s mark and Day 6 FRMI 1.1% worse, both because the stop was armed at
   an already-printed level and converted to a marketable sweep. The sim never
   pays that.
3. **Names missing from the ledger are missing from the pool.** The universe is
   the ledger's own crossers; live saw scan rows it never named. Missing names
   can only reduce the sim's opportunity set.
4. **No coverage gaps or outages.** The sim scans a perfect 07:00-14:30 window.
   Live lost 07:00-07:38 and 07:53-08:43 on Day 5, 07:00-07:20 on Day 6, and
   **Day 6 was truncated by a 4.5-hour internet outage from 10:35 ET — tickets
   2-7 ($85,002) were undeployable for most of the session.** Day 6 is the ONE
   day where the backtest beat live (+$970 vs −$267 in run A), and the outage is
   a legitimate part of that. Even crediting live nothing for Day 6, the
   three-day comparison stays negative for the backtest.

**Favouring live (the backtest is handicapped here):**

5. **Ticket sizing.** The sim caps a ticket at 20% of trailing 10-minute volume
   (`max_vol_frac=0.2`, `vol_frac_window=10`, causal). Live used flat $15k
   tickets and additionally cut size on L2 depth. On thin names the sim deploys
   less than the $15k the table assumes.
6. **The pool is richer than the champion's measurement basis.** See §5; run B
   controls for this.
7. **Day 8's bars are incomplete.** Polygon's free tier still 403s on same-day
   data, so 08-13 uses the partial Robinhood pulls: 59 pool names, only 31 with
   pre-07:00 bars (so `gap7` is uncomputable for the rest and they are gated
   out), median 25 bars in the 07:00-15:00 window. Day 8's backtest number is
   unreliable in both directions and is reported separately for that reason as
   well.
8. **Names without bars cannot occupy rank 0**, so a halal name reaches rank 0
   more often in the sim than live and gets the 35% calm-gap grace more often.
   This makes the sim more permissive, not less.

## 5. The two findings that matter more than the P&L gap

### 5a. The benchmark was measured on a pool the live halal gate can rarely buy

`gain_pct` in the gappers files is the **DAY-HIGH gain** (I computed it the same
way here, for shape compatibility). The m1 bar cache backing the champion's
$665,667 was backfilled by *day-high gain depth* — walk-8 / top-12 / walk-16,
median ~17 names/day with bars. Ranking the reconstructed pools by that same
day-high gain:

| Date | pool | halal-list members | of which inside top-16 by day gain |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| 08-10 | 124 | 23 | **2** (SLN, BCAR) |
| 08-11 | 68 | 12 | **3** (HQI, ALMR, FF) |
| 08-12 | 113 | 36 | **1** (ZTG) |
| 08-13 | 59 | 6 | 3 (HLIT, CHCI, NCMI) |

The names live actually traded rank **80th of 124 (LFST), 23rd of 68 (FRMI) and
42nd of 113 (BE)** by day-high gain. They were almost certainly never in the
measurement basis at all. The halal gate systematically kills the monsters — Day
5's top runner SCKT (+617%) failed on loans at 255% of a $3.2M mcap, Day 7's
BOXL (+237%) was a seed FAIL — leaving the modest +10-18% names that the m1
backfill never reached. **The $1,541/traded-day benchmark is measured on a
different population than the one we can trade.**

### 5b. The backtest's halal gate and the live halal gate are different functions

`axb.halal_pt` fell through to the **conservative Massive-bounds path** for these
names (all liabilities counted as debt, all current assets as cash). It therefore
**refused LFST, FRMI, SLN and NESR — names live screened and passed** on actual
quarterly debt/cash (LFST: loans 9.74%, cash 4.79%, combined 14.53%). In the
other direction it **passed CAVA, HYLN, HP, HPK and KOPN**, which today's live
screen refuses.

Neither gate is a superset of the other. The consequence is blunt: **C37's
$665,667 was earned under a halal gate we do not trade.** That is a
benchmark-validity problem independent of anything in this replay, and it is
larger than the P&L question that prompted it.

## 6. Ranked reasons for the gap

Ranked by size, gap measured on days 5-7 as backtest-minus-live:

1. **Halal-gate mismatch — worth about −$3,170** (run A −$1,527 → run C −$4,698
   once today's screen is enforced). The backtest's profit on these days is
   concentrated in names we would refuse. This is the dominant term and it runs
   *against* the hypothesis.
2. **Rotation into worse names — roughly −$1,300 to −$1,400.** Run B, which
   matches the champion's own coverage depth and uses only the point-in-time
   gate, is −$1,422 against live's −$228 with 7 tickets against live's 3. More
   deployment produced more loss. On 08-10 the sim's rotation ladder took five
   tickets and lost $2,588 while live's single LFST position lost $66.
3. **Entry timing on shared names — about −$460 on the one overlapping name.**
   BE: live +$105, sim −$353. The 5-minute re-rank cadence and the wait for a
   rank-1 slot cost the sim a $235.37 entry that live's armed premarket-high
   stop-buy caught at 09:26.

Working *for* live and against the hypothesis in the other direction, worth
roughly +$1,240 on Day 6 alone: the outage genuinely removed rotation capacity
live would otherwise have had. That is the only component of the comparison that
supports "money left on the table", and it is an availability failure, not a
gating or execution failure.

## 7. How the inputs were built (all of it had to be constructed)

There were no minute bars for these dates and the gappers pools end 2026-07-31.

**Universe** — `plan/replay_0810_pool.py`. Every ticker-like token in
`data/paper_days/{date}.{json,md}` was collected (the ledgers name their crossers
incrementally through the cycle log), then kept only if Polygon's daily bar for
that date shows a real **day-high gain ≥ +10%** over the prior session's close
with close ≥ $2 — the live scanner's own filters (Last > $2, %Change > 0.10). The
ledger is the source of truth for who crossed; the Polygon check only removes
prose words that happen to be tickers. Result: **124 / 68 / 113 / 59 crossers**.
Spot-checked prev_close against the ledgers: LFST 10.84, FRMI 5.88, BE 211.21 —
all exact.

**gain_pct is the DAY-HIGH gain**, computed the same way as the existing
`gappers_novol_*.json` files, and carried only for shape compatibility. Under
C37H (`causal_pool=True`) it is not used to select the pool.

**Bars** — 08-10/11/12 from Massive/Polygon 1-minute aggregates, written to
`data/massive/m1/{SYM}_{DATE}.csv` byte-compatible with the existing cache (UTC
`begins_at` index, Open/High/Low/Close/Volume). **Coverage: 124/124, 68/68,
113/113 — 100%.** 08-13 is not yet in the free tier (403 on same-day), so it uses
the 61 Robinhood CSVs already in `data/rh_bars/`; coverage there is 59 of the
ledger's 103 crossers, and the 44 missing are all names the ledger recorded as
halal FAIL or never screened — **no halal-eligible Day 8 name is missing bars**.

**Halal caches** — `plan/replay_0810_halal_warm.py` pre-fetched point-in-time
shares and Massive financials for all 323 pool symbols, 0 failures. It fetches
through the same throttled client `halal_pt` would use, with a poisoning guard
the stock helpers lack (they cache `{}` on a failed call, pinning a symbol to its
static verdict forever). 34 symbols reused an already-cached share count from a
month ≤ the trade month (max 6 months back): strictly backward-looking, so no
future leak, and conservative — an older, smaller share count gives a smaller
mcap and therefore *larger* ratios, i.e. a stricter gate.

**Filing lag** — `axb.FILING_LAG_DAYS = 45` as `rotation_sim.py` sets it.

**No lookahead.** `day_candidates` and `rank_at` were used unmodified; a name is
eligible only after its +10% cross has printed, and ranking at time *t* reads
bars ≤ *t* only. No existing config or champion logic was changed; everything
here is additive.
