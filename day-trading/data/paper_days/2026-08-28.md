| Time | Spread | Depth |
|---|---|---|
| 10:01 | **FAIL** 0.631–0.945% | **FAIL** 875 sh (18.7%) |

### Tick-size floor confirmed on a second name (Day-17 finding)

At $3.17 one $0.01 tick is **0.315%** of price. The 0.50% cap therefore admits
**at most a 1-tick spread** on TII — spread compliance is a near-binary test,
not a continuous one. For a 2-tick spread to fit the cap the stock must be
≥ $4.00. This structurally inflates the veto rate on sub-$4 names.

**Catch rate ~14% (1 of 7)** at a 5–8 minute poll against a 2-minute freshness
window. This is **not** a tagging failure — the tags refused every stale signal
correctly. It is a **cadence failure, and it is mine**: the protocol mandates
every-minute polling of the current TOP name, and I ran 5–8 minutes because a
true 1-minute poll costs ~500 tool calls across the session.

**Did it cost money today? Almost certainly not, and that is checkable rather
than assumed:** displayed depth was 4–19% of a full ticket at every measurement,
so the six missed signals would have met the same refusal. The gap cost
*observations*, not fills. **On a name whose book could absorb a ticket it would
mean discarding six of every seven legal Trigger C entries** — and Trigger C is
one of only three legal entries.

Loop ordering was correct throughout (Day-13 rule): fetch → append → trigger →
**read the tag** → then wait. No wait was ever inserted before reading a tag.

### Data-hygiene item found at the close

RH's own bar aggregations disagree on TII's post-arm high: **3.210** on 1-minute
bars vs **3.1891** on the 30-minute bar covering the same window. An aggregate
should be the maximum of its constituents. Both sit below the trigger so today's
no-fill verdict is unaffected, and the **higher** (more conservative) figure was
cited — but a live resting-stop test that trusted the coarser aggregation would
**under-detect intrabar touches**, which is exactly the failure the intrabar
semantic exists to prevent. Worth checking before any stop logic reads anything
coarser than 1-minute.
